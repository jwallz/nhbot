State budget & revenue

Where New Hampshire's state government gets its money and where it spends it — the whole state's finances, separate from the town-by-town property taxes elsewhere on this site. New Hampshire is unusual: it funds its budget with almost no broad income or sales tax, leaning instead on business taxes and the meals & rooms tax.

Most of it isn't money the state can spend freely — it's tied to specific programs. The single biggest piece is the federal share of Medicaid: Washington reimburses roughly half of what New Hampshire spends on health coverage for lower-income residents, children, seniors, and people with disabilities. That flows through the Department of Health & Human Services, which alone receives {{ dhhs.amount|usd }} — about {{ dhhs.pct }}% of all federal funds. The rest is largely federal highway aid and education grants (Title I, special education, school meals) passed through to local districts.

State tax & fee revenue FY{{ revenue_year }}

Unrestricted General & Education Fund revenue by source, with the change from FY{{ revenue_prior }}. This is the money the state raises itself.

New Hampshire has no broad-based income or general sales tax. Its largest single source is business taxes, followed by the statewide property tax, the lottery transfer, the meals & rooms (rentals) tax, and insurance and real-estate-transfer taxes.
